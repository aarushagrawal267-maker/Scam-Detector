## 2. Regeneron ISEF rules for human participants (these apply to IRIS too)

Source: [Society for Science: Human Participants rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/) and the [2027 Rules index](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-and-guidelines), checked 2026-11-17.

Verified points:

- Research with human participants *"must be reviewed and approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) before any interaction (e.g., recruitment, data collection) with human participants may begin."*
  - **Research done before approval cannot be used.**
- **Product testing counts as human-participant research.** If anyone other than you tests your prototype, or you survey people about it, you need IRB pre-approval. That covers your usability test with older adults and your before/after awareness survey.
- **Form 4 (Human Participants Form):** the school IRB records its risk decision on it.
- **Consent:**
  - Participation starts only after voluntary informed consent.
  - Adults consent for themselves.
  - Minors need their own assent plus **written parental permission**.
  - Participants must be told they can stop at any time.
- **IRB membership:** at least three people:
  - an educator who is *not* your Adult Sponsor,
  - a school administrator (preferably the principal or vice principal),
  - a medical or mental-health professional.
- **Privacy:**
  - You may not publish anything that identifies participants without written consent.
  - The research plan must say whether data are anonymous or confidential, where they are stored, who can access them, and what happens to them afterwards.
- **Changes** to the research plan need new approval before work continues.

**What this means for you:**
- **Model training and evaluation on public datasets** are not human-participant research (no interaction with people).
- **Collecting scam messages from real people**, **usability testing** and **workshop surveys** *are*. Get IRB approval for all of them first.
- `docs/PILOT_PROTOCOL.md` should be the document you take to your school's IRB.

## 3. Journal of Emerging Investigators (JEI)

Sources: [JEI FAQ](https://emerginginvestigators.org/submissions/faq), [Author eligibility](https://emerginginvestigators.org/submissions/author-eligibility), [Submission guide](https://emerginginvestigators.org/submissions/guidelines), checked 2026-11-17.

- **Eligibility (verified):**
  - *"All middle- and high-school students, aged 13 or older"*.
  - You must submit before you enrol in university.
  - Submissions from outside the US are accepted (in English).
- **An adult must submit the manuscript** (verified). You need a mentor: a teacher or researcher.
- **Cost (verified):** *"$49 fee at the time of submission"*, no publication fee, and a waiver can be requested.
- **Hypothesis-driven (verified):** papers that *"describe an invention"* are commonly returned.
  - Frame the paper as an experiment, e.g. *"H1: a multilingual transformer achieves higher recall than TF-IDF + logistic regression on Hinglish scam messages."*
  - Don't frame it as "I built an app".
- **Human subjects (verified):** you must include documentation of ethics approval.
- **Review time:** not stated. It varies by time of year.
- **Fit:** good for the model-comparison study (Phase 9 of the master plan).

## 4. National High School Journal of Science (NHSJS)

Source: [NHSJS submission guidelines](https://nhsjs.com/submission-guidelines/), checked 2026-11-17.

- **Verified:**
  - Research papers and systematic reviews accepted.
  - At most 20 pages, 12-pt font, single spacing.
  - Abstract 200–250 words.
  - Methods must state *"any ethical concerns… including informed consent, confidentiality."*
- **NOT VERIFIED on the official site:**
  - Eligibility: third-party sites say grades 9–12 worldwide.
  - Fees: third-party sites conflict between free and about $250.
  - Review time.
  - Read the official "Submission Types", "Peer Review Process" and "AI Usage Policy" pages before submitting.
- **AI usage:** NHSJS has an AI-usage policy page. Read it, and **disclose honestly** that AI tools helped build the code.

## Honesty rules for all competitions

- **Only enter work you can explain line by line.** Judges and reviewers will ask about your threshold choice, your train/test split and why you didn't use accuracy alone.
- **Disclose AI assistance** where the rules ask for it (several journals now have AI-use policies).
- **Never present synthetic test results as real-world accuracy.**
- **Never report pilot data collected before IRB approval.**
