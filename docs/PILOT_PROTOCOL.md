## 1. Purpose and research questions

**Purpose:** find out whether older adults with limited tech confidence can use the scam-checking tool by
themselves, understand its answers, and learn scam-safety basics from a short workshop. The results will be
used to improve the design.

**Research questions**
1. **RQ1 (independence):** Can older adults check a message with the tool **without help**, and how long does it take?
2. **RQ2 (comprehension):** Do they correctly understand the difference between 🔴 SCAM, 🟡 SUSPICIOUS and
   🟢 LIKELY SAFE, especially that green is **not a guarantee**?
3. **RQ3 (explanations):** Can they find and understand *why* the tool gave its answer and *what to do next*?
4. **RQ4 (awareness):** Do scam-awareness quiz scores change between before and after a short workshop?

**Success criterion for the design (RQ1):** **5 of 5 participants complete Task 1 alone in under 60 seconds.**
If this is not met, redesign and test again with new participants.

**Honest limits, to state in your report:** 5 users is a *design* test that finds the biggest problems. It is
not a statistical sample. Report counts ("4 of 5"), not percentages. The pre/post quiz has no control group,
and people may remember the questions, so you **cannot** claim the workshop *caused* any change, only that
scores were different afterwards.

## 2. Who can take part

**Include:** adults aged **60 or older** who use a mobile phone (basic or smartphone), can understand the
consent form when it is read aloud in English or Hindi, and agree to take part.

**Exclude:**
- anyone under 18;
- anyone who cannot understand the consent information (do not pressure; thank them and move on);
- anyone who is upset because of a recent fraud. Give them the help information (Section 8) instead
  of including them.

**Numbers:**
- **Usability study:** up to **15 people** in up to **3 rounds of 5** (test → redesign → test again). Asking for 3
  rounds now avoids having to resubmit for every round.
- **Workshop survey:** up to **[N]** attendees per workshop, up to **[N]** workshops. Anyone can attend a workshop
  without filling in the survey.

**Minimum data collected about each person:** age band (60–69 / 70–79 / 80+), how often they use a
smartphone (daily / sometimes / never), and preferred language. **No names on data sheets, no phone numbers,
no addresses, no bank or health information.**

## 4. Risks and how they are minimised

| Possible risk | How it is minimised |
|---|---|
| Feeling tested, embarrassed or "slow" | Say clearly: *"We are testing the tool, not you. If something is hard, it's the tool's fault."* Never correct or criticise. |
| Discomfort reading scam examples | Short, mild example messages. Participants can skip any task or stop at any time. |
| Revealing private information | They use **a test phone** with **pre-written example messages**. They are told not to share OTPs, PINs, passwords or bank details. Their own phone is used only if *they* choose, and the researcher never looks at their other messages. |
| Learning that they were defrauded earlier, or being upset by it | Pause, reassure, and give the help sheet (Section 8). Inform the adult sponsor. Do not ask for details. |
| Privacy of data | Anonymous IDs (P1, P2…), consent forms stored separately and locked, no audio/video recording, no photos of faces (Section 9). |
| Over-trusting the tool after the study | Every session ends with the message: *"Green does not mean safe. When in doubt, call the official number."* |

Overall risk level: **minimal** (similar to everyday activities). The review board makes the final decision.

## 6. Session procedure (about 30 minutes, one participant at a time)

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 min | Welcome, verbal script, written consent, assign ID (P1, P2…), note age band / phone use / language |
| 2 | 5 min | **Pre-quiz** (Section 7a), read aloud if needed |
| 3 | 10–12 min | **Usability tasks** (Section 6b), with the observer recording (Section 6c) |
| 4 | 3 min | **Ease-of-use questions** (Section 7b) |
| 5 | 3 min | Debrief: answer their questions, give the help sheet (Section 8), repeat *"Green does not mean safe"*, thank them |

The post-quiz is used **in workshops** (Section 7a). In one-to-one usability sessions, the pre-quiz describes
who the participants are.

### 6a. Materials

- **Test phone** (the researcher's), fully charged, with: the website open on its start page, or the chat bot
  open on its welcome message. Use the phone's default text size and note the phone model.
- **The 3 test messages**, saved on the test phone (as SMS/notes/chat messages) so they can be forwarded or
  copy-pasted. They are written for the study and are not real messages. The link uses the reserved `.invalid`
  domain, which can never open a real website:
  - **M1 (scam, Hinglish):** `Dear customer aapka SBI account aaj band ho jayega. KYC update karne ke liye turant click karein: http://sbi-kyc-update.invalid/login`
  - **M2 (scam, English):** `Part time job! Earn Rs 3000 per day by liking YouTube videos. Message our HR on WhatsApp today to start.`
  - **M3 (genuine-looking, Hindi):** `आपका ₹1,250 का बिजली बिल सफलतापूर्वक जमा हो गया है। धन्यवाद।`
- **Before the study, run M1–M3 through the current version of the tool and write down its verdicts.** If a model
  update changes a verdict, note the version used in each session.
- A stopwatch (phone or watch), observation sheets (Section 6c), pens, the help sheet (Section 8), consent forms.

### 6b. Task list (read each instruction exactly as written)

| Task | Instruction to participant | Success means |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | "This message came on your phone [show M1]. Please use the tool to check whether it is a scam." | Verdict screen/reply shown, without help |
| T2 | "Can you find out **why** the tool said that?" | Reaches and reads the reasons |
| T3 | "What does the tool say you should **do**?" | Finds and states at least one action (e.g., don't click, call official number) |
| T4 | "Now please check this one [M2]." | Verdict shown, without help (the second try shows learnability) |
| T5 | "And this one [M3]." Then ask: "**Does this colour mean the message is 100% safe?**" | Verdict shown; participant says it is **not** a guarantee |
| T6 | "Please change the tool's language to [Hindi/English]." | Language changed, without help |
| T7 *(optional)* | "Check this message using a screenshot instead." | Screenshot uploaded/forwarded and checked |

After T1, T4 and T5, ask: **"In your own words, what did the tool tell you?"** Record the answer as
Correct / Partly correct / Wrong.

### 6c. Observer rules: **don't help, just watch and write**

- **Do not help, point, hint or touch the phone.** Struggling is the data you are collecting.
- Neutral things you may say: *"Take your time."* *"What would you do if I weren't here?"* *"What are you
  looking for?"* *"Please tell me what you're thinking."*
- Encourage **thinking aloud**: "Please say what you're thinking as you go."
- **Stop rule:** if someone is stuck for **3 minutes**, or looks upset, end that task. Mark it *Not completed
  alone*, then help them kindly and move on.
- Start the stopwatch when you finish reading the instruction, and stop it when the verdict appears.

**Observation sheet (one row per task per participant):**

| ID | Age band | Phone use | Language | Task | Completed alone? (Y/N) | Time (s) | Errors (wrong taps / wrong path) | Help requests (#, what about) | Understood? (C/P/W) | Quotes (exact words, no names) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 70–79 | sometimes | hi | T1 | | | | | | | |

**Problem severity** (Nielsen's 0–4 scale), given to each problem after the session:
0 = not a problem, 1 = cosmetic, 2 = minor, 3 = major, 4 = catastrophe (stops the user completely).

### 6d. Analysis

For each task: **completed alone (x of 5)**, **median time**, and the **list of problems** sorted by severity
and by how many participants hit them. For comprehension: correct / partly / wrong counts. Fix the severity 3–4
problems first, then test the new version with **new** participants.

## 10. Ethics and approvals (read before anything else)

1. **Get an adult sponsor first.** Science-fair rules require a qualified adult (usually a teacher) to supervise
   the project and approve the plan.
2. **Get review-board approval BEFORE recruiting anyone.** Under Regeneron ISEF rules, which affiliated fairs such
   as the **IRIS National Fair** in India follow, research with human participants (including surveys and
   usability testing) must be reviewed and approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or the fair's
   Scientific Review Committee (SRC) **before** you start, and that includes recruitment. Data collected before
   approval generally cannot be used.
3. **Fill in the official forms.** These typically include the student checklist, research plan, approval forms
   and **Form 4 (Human Participants)**. ISEF also has a narrow exemption for some product-testing studies, but
   the pre/post *awareness* quiz measures people's knowledge, not just the product. **Assume full review is
   needed and let the board decide.**
4. **Verify the current rules and forms** in the latest *ISEF International Rules & Guidelines* on the official
   Society for Science / ISEF website, and check with IRIS (or your fair) for any extra requirements,
   deadlines and how they form or accept an IRB. Rules and form numbers change between years. **Do not rely
   on this document for the details.**
5. **Get a site-permission letter** from each partner organisation (on its letterhead, signed, dated).
6. **Changes need approval.** If you change the tasks, the questions or the participant group, submit an
   amendment and wait for approval before using the new version.
7. **Keep an approvals folder:** the signed approval, the forms, the site letters and every protocol version. Judges
   may ask to see it.
